# Can Cats Find Their Way Home?

We were out early enough that morning that I made a decision I don't usually make: I'd let him lead. Fully. No hindrance, no redirecting, no executive decisions from me. Just follow.

This is one story about Cheeto, not proof that every cat can safely navigate home. He was supervised, leashed, and walking familiar ground with me the entire time.

## Following Cheeto's route

We were between the housing development and the store strip, out on the trail and road part, not down in the wash. And he took it seriously. Every tree got investigated. Every bush. At one point he led us straight off trail into a messy cactus garden, and I trailed around the edges while he wove through like he'd mapped it years ago. We had water. He'd stop and drink when he wanted to. There isn't much shade out there, but it was early enough that it didn't matter yet.

I was so locked into following him that I didn't notice, for a while, what he was actually doing. He wasn't exploring anymore. He was heading home.

## The route only a cat could use

It hit me when he stopped at a barbed wire fence and tried to go under it. And that's when I realized — our house was right there. Just on the other side of that fence, through a stretch of cactus garden that looked like God planted it there Himself. He knew exactly where home was. He'd probably known the whole time. He just took the most direct route that existed in his cat-sized mind, and that route went straight under a barbed wire fence built for something roughly one-tenth my size.

He fit. Obviously he fit. He's a cat, he fits through everything. I, on the other hand, am a full-grown woman. A tiny one, sure, but still not one who casually slides under barbed wire. I stood there doing the math on that in real time while my cat looked back at me like I was the one being unreasonable.

I tried to redirect him. Tried to get him to go a different way. Tried, more than once, to convince him into the backpack so I could just carry him around the long way, around the fence he had zero interest in respecting. He wasn't having any of it. He knew where home was. He had a perfectly good route, and as far as he was concerned the fact that I couldn't use it was my problem, not his. 

## When his way and mine could not both work

So we went around the way I could actually go — down off the beaten path, back onto the road — and he practically hurried me the whole way, up the driveway, straight to his favorite shady sandy spot, where he dropped down and rolled around in the dirt like he'd just finished exactly the walk he meant to.

I still smile when I think about that morning.

I set out thinking I was letting Cheeto explore, when really he already had a destination in mind. He knew where home was the whole time. He just happened to pick the one route that only a cat could use.

He was right all along.

His human just couldn't fit.
