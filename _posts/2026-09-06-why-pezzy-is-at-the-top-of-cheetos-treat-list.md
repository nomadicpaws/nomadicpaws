I knew before Cheeto ever tasted one.

I saw that Pezzy made pet treats from [lionfish](/cheetos-store/#lionfish-sticks), and something in me immediately said, *My cat is going to lose his mind over these.*

This wasn’t based on careful product testing or a sophisticated understanding of his palate. I just know my cat. Lionfish in treat form looked exactly like the kind of thing that would turn my ordinarily gentlemanly orange boy into someone with no respect for personal space.

I was right.

## The lionfish incident

“Enthusiastic” would be a polite description of Cheeto’s reaction. The moment he realized what I had, his manners disappeared. Waiting patiently was no longer part of his belief system. There was only Cheeto, the lionfish, and the terrible injustice of my hand standing between them.

That reaction matters to me because cats are honest reviewers. Cheeto doesn’t care about packaging, conservation language, or whether a product fits neatly into the Nomadic Paws story. He only knows whether he wants another bite.

He very much wanted another bite.

## Why these aren’t just another fish treat

Cheeto’s reaction got my attention, but Pezzy’s mission is what put the treats at the top of my list.

Pezzy makes single- and limited-ingredient treats for cats and dogs using invasive species such as [lionfish](/cheetos-store/#lionfish-sticks), [silver carp](/cheetos-store/#silver-carp-chips), and [devil fish](/cheetos-store/#devil-fish-strips). These animals can cause serious damage when they become established outside their native ecosystems. Removing them helps native wildlife—but removal on a meaningful scale requires people to have a reason to keep catching them.

Pezzy helps create that reason.

By turning invasive species into something useful, the company creates demand for animals we actually need removed. That supports fishers while giving native species and damaged ecosystems more room to recover. It takes an environmental problem and turns part of the solution into something Cheeto considers an emergency-level snack.

That is the kind of conservation message I can get behind.

## Simple treats for cats and dogs

The single-ingredient varieties are exactly what they sound like: one animal protein without a long list of fillers added around it. The [Lionfish Sticks](/cheetos-store/#lionfish-sticks) that destroyed Cheeto’s dignity are made from lionfish, and the [Silver Carp Chips](/cheetos-store/#silver-carp-chips) have also been a success in our house.

Cheeto may be the loudest member of the testing committee, but he isn’t the only one. The dogs enjoy Pezzy too, which makes the treats especially useful in a home where several different animals are always interested in whatever bag I just opened.

Still, Cheeto’s vote carries particular weight here. He can be enthusiastic about food, but this was something else. Pezzy didn’t merely pass his test. It temporarily removed his ability to behave like a civilized house cat.

## Why Pezzy earned the top spot

There are plenty of treats Cheeto will eat. Pezzy is at the top of my list because it gives me more than one reason to reach for the bag.

Cheeto genuinely loves it. The single-ingredient options are refreshingly straightforward. Both cats and dogs can enjoy them. Most importantly to me, each bag supports a model that gives people an economic reason to remove invasive species and protect the native wildlife affected by them.
