Packing for a hike with a cat sounds like it should require an impressive amount of specialized equipment. In reality, what comes with us is fairly simple: the gear that keeps Cheeto safely attached to me, enough water to support both of us, and a backpack that gives him somewhere secure to ride when four paws are no longer the best way home.

The items may be simple, but each one has earned its place through actual use. I’m not packing for every situation we could theoretically encounter. I’m packing for the situations Cheeto and I have already learned to expect.

## The harness and leash

Before anything else, Cheeto wears his [BuddyArmor harness and leash](/checklist/#buddyarmor-harness-leash). His first harness taught me just how easily a determined cat can back out of equipment that seems secure. The BuddyArmor harness we use now gives me much more confidence because of its secure fit while still allowing him to move comfortably on the trail.

The harness goes on before we leave, and the leash stays attached whenever he is outside. His recall is an additional layer of preparation, but it never replaces the physical connection between us.

I’ve written more about how we built those positive associations in [How to Leash Train a Cat: What Worked for Cheeto](/trail-journal/2026-08-09-how-to-leash-train-a-cat-what-worked-for-cheeto).

## The backpack that always comes with us

Cheeto may walk for most of an adventure, but his [Apollo Walker backpack carrier](/checklist/#apollo-walker-backpack) comes with us every time.

The backpack serves several purposes. It safely carries him through the transition from indoors to outdoors, gives him somewhere familiar to rest, and allows me to carry him home when distance, heat, or his own energy level makes walking the wrong choice.

Sometimes he rides willingly. Sometimes he remains deeply convinced that the adventure should continue in another direction. Either way, the backpack gives me a safe option when I need to make the decision for both of us.

It also carries my wallet, which means the backpack comes with me on plenty of ordinary outings too. As a result, Cheeto gets invited on far more little adventures than he would if his carrier were stored away and only brought out for planned hikes.

## Two bottles of water—every time

Water is the heaviest thing we carry and the last thing I would consider leaving behind.

I pack two bottles regardless of how long I expect the hike to be. One may be enough for the original plan, but trails have a way of becoming longer than expected—especially when the cat leading the expedition disagrees with the proposed route home.

Cheeto’s bowl stays clean and ready inside his backpack pocket. Whenever he asks for water, he gets it. He knows I carry it for him, and I believe that certainty is part of what helps him remain comfortable outside.

Our [collapsible water bottle and bowl setup](/checklist/#collapsible-water-bowl) keeps his water accessible without requiring me to unload the entire backpack whenever he needs a drink.

## The things that stay in the backpack

Some items live in Cheeto’s backpack rather than being gathered before every hike. His water bowl, leash, and other small essentials already have designated places, which makes forgetting them much less likely.

That is part of why our packing routine works: I’m not rebuilding the system from nothing every time we leave. The important pieces return to the same places after each adventure so they are ready for the next one.

My wallet stays zipped inside too. It may not be cat-hiking equipment, technically, but keeping it there means grabbing my wallet and grabbing Cheeto’s backpack are often the same action. Cheeto has benefited considerably from this arrangement.

## What we don’t pack just because it appears on the checklist

Cheeto’s complete trail-ready checklist includes more than what physically comes with us on every hike. The [EquiGroomer deshedding brush](/checklist/#equigroomer-deshedding-brush), [cat-safe pet wipes](/checklist/#cat-safe-paw-coat-wipes), [everyday Tiki Cat food](/checklist/#tiki-cat-born-carnivore), and other supplies support his life before or after the trail, but that doesn’t mean they all need to take up room in the backpack.

The packing list is intentionally smaller. If an item doesn’t help with hydration, safety, transportation, or a realistic need during that particular adventure, it can usually stay home.

That keeps the backpack manageable for me and comfortable for Cheeto when he needs to ride in it.

## Packing for the hike we might actually have

Most of our gear isn’t there to make the adventure look polished. It is there for the moment when the plan changes.

The second water bottle matters when we wander farther than expected. The backpack matters when Cheeto wants to continue but the desert sun says otherwise. The secure harness matters every second we are outside, including the uneventful ones.

That is what I’m really packing for—not the perfect version of the hike, but the real one.
